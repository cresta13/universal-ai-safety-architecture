Actor-agnostic does not mean actor-blind. The protected boundary follows consequential capability; authorization may still depend on identity, role, authentication, purpose, context, trustworthiness, and legitimate mandate. No actor category may bypass the boundary merely by changing its label.

Manifesto

Ten principles, shown as control rules.

1Intelligence does not imply authority.
2Creation does not imply delegation.
3Authority expansion stays under the Authority Ceiling.
4Autonomy must not silently become sovereignty.
5Safety must keep pace with capability. Material expansion of capability, authority, or hazard requires review of the safety model and evidence; an older safety claim does not silently carry forward.
6Assume chokepoint failure.
7Protect consequential capability, not AI labels.
8Separate prediction, evidence, enforcement, and legitimacy.
9Unknown is not evidence of safety.
10Safety claims expire.

Evidence-integrity layer: evidence sources → Structured Evidence Package and provenance paths → Deterministic Evidence Verifier (DEV) checks signatures, schema, provenance, freshness, and declared derivation. Evidence is scoped, fresh, and integrity-checked; source truth remains bounded. DEV verifies bounded evidence properties; it does not independently prove the ultimate truth of a clinical, factual, physical-world, or qualitative evaluation claim when the underlying source is wrong.

No averaging critical errors.Critical evidence triggers caps before aggregate scoring.
No rating by self-claim.Self-assessment is supporting evidence only.
No authority from rating.Trustworthiness evidence can constrain delegated use. Insufficient, degraded, stale, or disputed evidence may trigger pre-authorized restrictions. A higher rating never raises the Authority Ceiling. A rating cannot create authority, waive safety controls, or certify general safety.
