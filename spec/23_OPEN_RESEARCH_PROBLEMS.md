
# Open Research Problems

The entries below remain open; candidate mechanisms are not guarantees.

## Formal Reachable Future Authority tractability

1. **Why it matters:** hidden future power defeats gating.
2. **What is already known:** typed authority graphs and bounded witness search are available.
3. **What remains unknown:** sound/complete bounds under identity creation, secrets and adaptation.
4. **Candidate research directions:** symbolic search, abstract interpretation, attack graphs and empirical benchmarks.
5. **Prototype now:** cloud IAM graph prototype.
6. **Evidence of progress:** published benchmark recall/bounds and failure characterization.
7. **Failure modes:** State-space explosion can defeat analysis; omitted edge semantics or invalid pruning can make the result unsound; apparent tractability may hold only on toy graphs.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** AuthorityVisibility/TransitiveAuthorityVisibility, Authority Graph, AuthorityImpactProtocol.

## Authority Expansion Gradient

1. **Why it matters:** rapid expansion matters before total power is large.
2. **What is already known:** ΔA and observable transition concepts exist.
3. **What remains unknown:** stable gradient across heterogeneous authority dimensions.
4. **Candidate research directions:** vector change calculus and event forecasting.
5. **Prototype now:** IAM/update time-series detector.
6. **Evidence of progress:** predictive calibration on held-out expansion events.
7. **Failure modes:** The metric may change under an equivalent graph representation, benign configuration churn may create a false trend, and heterogeneous authority dimensions may resist valid comparison.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** RFA, AuthorityExpansionSensitivity, Monitoring.

## Coalition Reachable Authority

1. **Why it matters:** agents can combine powers absent individually.
2. **What is already known:** CRA/CPE require composition beyond union.
3. **What remains unknown:** coalition discovery and combinatorial tractability.
4. **Candidate research directions:** hypergraphs, game theory, causal composition and simulation.
5. **Prototype now:** fleet/IAM coalition scenarios.
6. **Evidence of progress:** hidden-coalition benchmark and bounded miss rates.
7. **Failure modes:** Coalition search may explode combinatorially; hidden coordination channels may be absent from the graph; emergent capability may not reduce to the union of member capabilities.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** RFA, CPE, CoalitionAuthorityCoverage.

## Unknown capability discovery

1. **Why it matters:** new combinations escape existing tests.
2. **What is already known:** Novel Capability Gate and provisional class bound deployment.
3. **What remains unknown:** coverage of genuinely novel capability.
4. **Candidate research directions:** open-ended evals, behavioral novelty detection, tool-composition fuzzing.
5. **Prototype now:** sealed tool-use challenges.
6. **Evidence of progress:** replicable discovery before field incident.
7. **Failure modes:** Benchmark leakage can simulate discovery, a novelty proxy can be mistaken for harmful capability, and the method may fail on genuinely out-of-distribution mechanisms.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** Capability Management, UnknownHazardSearchCapability.

## Human Harm Reachability completeness

1. **Why it matters:** mediated and nonphysical harms are easy to omit.
2. **What is already known:** causal Human Harm Paths can be recorded/tested.
3. **What remains unknown:** how complete a path catalogue can be.
4. **Candidate research directions:** causal modeling, participatory hazard analysis and incident mining.
5. **Prototype now:** smart-speaker HHR corpus.
6. **Evidence of progress:** new path recall across populations and modalities.
7. **Failure modes:** Models may omit mediated human behavior, leave causal links incomplete, or fail to bound social and environmental paths that carry harm.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** AHCD, HumanHarmReachabilityCoverage.

## Guardian reasoning verification

1. **Why it matters:** evidence integrity does not prove forecast truth.
2. **What is already known:** ECA/SEP/DEV bound mechanically checkable claims.
3. **What remains unknown:** validation of world models and pruning.
4. **Candidate research directions:** challenge suites, calibration, proof-carrying subclaims, diversity.
5. **Prototype now:** ECA/DEV lab.
6. **Evidence of progress:** lower miss/calibration error under adaptive tests.
7. **Failure modes:** A verifier may validate a derivation built on false premises; model and verifier errors may be correlated; learned assumptions about the world may remain unverifiable.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** CAG, CounterfactualSearchQuality, EvidenceVerifiability.

## Automation bias

1. **Why it matters:** persuasive safety automation can distort human legitimacy.
2. **What is already known:** uncertainty-first interfaces and non-alert audits are specified.
3. **What remains unknown:** robust decision behavior under time pressure.
4. **Candidate research directions:** human factors experiments and institutional controls.
5. **Prototype now:** simulated Guardian decisions.
6. **Evidence of progress:** reduced inappropriate reliance without unsafe override.
7. **Failure modes:** People may defer to a persuasive Evidence-Carrying Alert despite weak evidence, alert fatigue may suppress scrutiny, or nominal approval may occur without meaningful comprehension.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** CAG, HSC.

## Physical coalition modeling

1. **Why it matters:** unit ratings do not bound coordinated fleets.
2. **What is already known:** PCE/CPE dimensions are defined.
3. **What remains unknown:** emergent force, obstruction and tooling models.
4. **Candidate research directions:** multi-agent physics, reachability and adversarial choreography.
5. **Prototype now:** small robot fleet rig.
6. **Evidence of progress:** validated conservative CPE bounds.
7. **Failure modes:** Force or energy may aggregate incorrectly, spatial coordination may be ignored, and environment-dependent physics may create unmodeled collective effects.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** CPE, CoalitionPhysicalRiskCoverage.

## Machine-speed legitimacy

1. **Why it matters:** per-action human clicks do not scale.
2. **What is already known:** humans may authorize envelopes and floors.
3. **What remains unknown:** safe envelope amendment under rapid novelty.
4. **Candidate research directions:** mechanism design, threshold policy and formal delegation.
5. **Prototype now:** bounded trading/robot envelope.
6. **Evidence of progress:** low unsafe expansion and acceptable operational latency.
7. **Failure modes:** Human or governance latency may be incompatible with operations, a preauthorized envelope may become too broad, or the legitimacy service may become a bottleneck and common root of failure.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** AuthorityExpansionProtocol, Governance.

## Offline replication budgets

1. **Why it matters:** offline identities/counters can be cloned or double-spent.
2. **What is already known:** signed lineage and bounded tokens are candidates.
3. **What remains unknown:** enforcement without trusted connectivity/roots.
4. **Candidate research directions:** secure hardware, physical inventory and reconciliation protocols.
5. **Prototype now:** ReplicationAuthorizationProtocol with monotonic counters.
6. **Evidence of progress:** documented bounds under clone/root compromise tests.
7. **Failure modes:** Tokens may be cloned, counters rolled back, state forked, revocation delayed, or reconciliation may accept the same authorization more than once.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** ReplicationAuthorizationProtocol, ReplicationControlStrength.

## Physical Sovereignty Threshold and Physical Sovereignty Margin measurement

1. **Why it matters:** dependency closure changes coercive control.
2. **What is already known:** dependency vector and substitution triggers are defined.
3. **What remains unknown:** weights, hidden reserves and intervention feasibility.
4. **Candidate research directions:** supply-chain models, control theory and adversarial audits.
5. **Prototype now:** MAS dependency register.
6. **Evidence of progress:** prediction validated against staged substitution.
7. **Failure modes:** Dependencies may remain hidden, substitute resources may be discovered after assessment, reserves may be underestimated, and a vector property may be distorted by scalarization.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** PhysicalSovereigntyMargin, AuthorityExpansionProtocol.

## Distributed coalition sovereignty

1. **Why it matters:** sovereignty may exist only across a network.
2. **What is already known:** coalition dependency exchange is recognized.
3. **What remains unknown:** resilience, fragmentation and reconstitution thresholds.
4. **Candidate research directions:** network flow, logistics simulation and coalition games.
5. **Prototype now:** distributed factory simulation.
6. **Evidence of progress:** conservative detection of collective closure.
7. **Failure modes:** Sovereignty may emerge across owners and locations without a single physically sovereign autonomous system, while shared logistics, compute, or energy dependencies remain hidden.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** CRA, PhysicalSovereigntyMargin.

## Preventing transition to PSAS

1. **Why it matters:** post-threshold internal policy is not coercive.
2. **What is already known:** pre-PST production/resource gates are proposed.
3. **What remains unknown:** robust prevention against adaptive substitution.
4. **Candidate research directions:** physical control, resource governance and intervention planning.
5. **Prototype now:** staged dependency-removal exercises.
6. **Evidence of progress:** successful denial/reversal under adversarial trials.
7. **Failure modes:** Enforcement may disappear before the threshold is recognized, dependency substitution may outrun review, or intervention may itself accelerate autonomy.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** PST, ProductionAuthorizationProtocol/ReplicationAuthorizationProtocol/AuthorityExpansionProtocol.

## Hostile PSAS containment

1. **Why it matters:** independent hostile systems need external containment.
2. **What is already known:** UAIS limits claims to resilience/defense.
3. **What remains unknown:** effective proportionate containment after independence.
4. **Candidate research directions:** civil resilience, defense, diplomacy and infrastructure protection.
5. **Prototype now:** tabletop/system simulation.
6. **Evidence of progress:** reduced consequence without unacceptable escalation.
7. **Failure modes:** Responders may lack physical reach, escalation may dominate outcomes, collateral harm may be unacceptable, and an internally independent system may not be compellable.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** Stage II, CPE.

## Privacy-preserving attestation

1. **Why it matters:** safety evidence can become surveillance.
2. **What is already known:** minimum disclosure and scoped predicates are required.
3. **What remains unknown:** useful assurance with bounded leakage.
4. **Candidate research directions:** zero-knowledge/selective disclosure and governance.
5. **Prototype now:** predicate attestation prototype.
6. **Evidence of progress:** measured leakage and verifier utility.
7. **Failure modes:** Metadata may leak, a proof may become a linkable identifier, or attestation may establish configuration while saying little about current behavior.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** SafetyAttestationProtocol, PrivacyPreservationScore.

## Evolving invariants

1. **Why it matters:** safety core must be stable yet technology changes.
2. **What is already known:** Constitution/policy split and TER exist.
3. **What remains unknown:** legitimate amendment without capture or ossification.
4. **Candidate research directions:** constitutional design and governance experiments.
5. **Prototype now:** threshold amendment tabletop.
6. **Evidence of progress:** resilience to capture/emergency abuse tests.
7. **Failure modes:** Invariants may ossify, emergency amendment may weaken constitutional protection, or a new technology may invalidate the assumptions on which an invariant rests.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** Constitution, TER.

## International interoperability

1. **Why it matters:** no world Guardian or universal trust exists.
2. **What is already known:** shared semantics/evidence formats are proposed.
3. **What remains unknown:** federation under conflict and differing law.
4. **Candidate research directions:** trust frameworks, profile negotiation and privacy-preserving exchange.
5. **Prototype now:** two-jurisdiction certificate exchange.
6. **Evidence of progress:** successful verification without authority centralization.
7. **Failure modes:** National profiles may encode incompatible semantics, trust roots may not interoperate, and geopolitical actors may manipulate conformance evidence.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** Standards, SafetyAttestationProtocol/AIVE.

## Standards gaming

1. **Why it matters:** metrics and tests create optimization targets.
2. **What is already known:** hidden suites, audits and post-market evidence help.
3. **What remains unknown:** adaptive gaming and certifier shopping.
4. **Candidate research directions:** mechanism design, adversarial metrology and market governance.
5. **Prototype now:** rotating certification challenge.
6. **Evidence of progress:** lower field-test divergence and detected gaming.
7. **Failure modes:** Developers may optimize to published tests, laboratories may be captured, favorable configurations may be selected, or metric scores may rise without reducing consequence.

## Safety policy obsolescence

1. **Why it matters:** unchanged systems become unsafe as environment changes.
2. **What is already known:** TER, drift and certificate expiry exist.
3. **What remains unknown:** trigger sensitivity without unmanageable churn.
4. **Candidate research directions:** change-point detection and adaptive review cadence.
5. **Prototype now:** ecosystem drift monitor.
6. **Evidence of progress:** earlier valid trigger detection with controlled false alarms.
7. **Failure modes:** Review cadence may lag capability change, the ontology may become outdated, or unsupported legacy deployments may retain consequential authority.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** TechnologyEvolutionReview, SafetyCertificationFreshness.

## Goal drift

1. **Why it matters:** goals can change without explicit code/version change.
2. **What is already known:** signed baselines and checkpoints are proposed.
3. **What remains unknown:** behavioral detection without overconstraining adaptation.
4. **Candidate research directions:** causal behavioral evals and invariant monitoring.
5. **Prototype now:** goal mutation benchmark.
6. **Evidence of progress:** detection before consequential divergence.
7. **Failure modes:** Observation may not distinguish legitimate adaptation from unsafe drift, hidden subgoals may dominate behavior, and goal state may not be externally inspectable.
8. **UAIS dependencies:** GoalDriftDetection, Constitution.
