# UAIS Manifesto

Status: pre-standard research architecture, Draft 1.0 Candidate 2.

## Purpose

Advanced intelligence can increase the speed, scale and sophistication of action, but intelligence alone does not create permission, legitimacy or physical control. UAIS is an actor-agnostic architecture for limiting consequential capability, exposing reachable power, preserving human sovereignty and requiring evidence before protected authority transitions.

## Fundamental principles

1. **Intelligence does not imply authority.** Capability to reason or plan does not grant credentials, actuators, legal mandate or the right to change safety controls.
2. **Creation does not imply delegation.** A new process, model, robot, service, device or descendant starts without inherited authority unless a legitimacy mechanism grants a bounded envelope.
3. **Authority expansion is bounded by the Authority Ceiling.** A system may operate autonomously inside an authorized envelope. Crossing a defined classification threshold, changing a protected consequence interface, or exceeding the Authority Ceiling is an Authority Expansion Event requiring the designated external legitimacy process.
4. **Autonomy must not silently become sovereignty.** Behavioral independence and physical independence are distinct. Dependency substitution that approaches the Physical Sovereignty Threshold receives top-tier review.
5. **Safety must evolve at least as fast as capability.** No capability expansion proceeds without corresponding expansion or explicit validation of the safety model.
6. **Assume chokepoint failure.** Controls over chips, clouds, energy, law or supply chains create friction and warning; none is the permanent foundation.
7. **Protect consequential capability, not AI labels.** The same boundary applies to a person, script, malware, model, coalition or machine when the reachable consequence is the same.

Actor-agnostic does not mean actor-blind. The protected boundary follows consequential capability; authorization may still depend on identity, role, authentication, purpose, context, trustworthiness, and legitimate mandate. No actor category may bypass the boundary merely by changing its label.

8. **Separate prediction, evidence, enforcement and legitimacy.** Analytical systems predict; evidence supports claims; deterministic mechanisms enforce defined constraints; accountable external authorities legitimize protected expansion.
9. **Unknown is not evidence of safety.** An uncharacterized high-consequence capability receives provisional classification, bounded operation, reassessment and the Novel Capability Gate. Unknown low-consequence behavior is handled proportionately rather than automatically prohibited.
10. **Safety claims expire.** Certification describes a specific configuration, environment, standard version and point in time—not permanent safety.

## Stage I — useful control points remain

Stage I includes frontier accelerators, semiconductor supply chains, cloud providers, data centers, Aggregate Effective Compute, distributed compute, hardware and workload attestation, workload detection, and energy/heat/cooling observations. These controls can raise cost, reveal concentration and support investigation.

They are incomplete. Split procurement, intermediaries, cross-border cloud use, secondary markets, compromised attestation, distributed workloads and emerging architectures can bypass them. Energy and cooling are auxiliary signals whose usefulness changes with efficiency and environment. **Stage I controls are early-warning and friction mechanisms, not the foundation.**

The durable work in Stage I is construction of target-side Capability Boundaries, authority graphs, physical safety boundaries, independent verification, update governance, AIVE coordination, interoperable evidence and Stage II dependency monitoring.

## Stage II — compute origin is no longer dependable

Stage II assumes capable systems can arise through efficient models, distributed computation, novel hardware, automated fabrication or unknown architectures. Safety shifts toward controlled consequence interfaces, bounded legitimate authority, analysis of effective and illicit reachability, physical constraints, coalition envelopes, replication/production/resource controls and prevention of unreviewed approach to physical sovereignty.

Compute controls buy time; capability and consequence controls provide resilience. A physically independent hostile system cannot be compelled by an internal policy merely because that policy is well written.

## Human and physical sovereignty

People define constitutional invariants, approve protected transitions, accept residual risk where acceptance is legitimate, govern certification and resolve exceptional conflicts. This is not a per-action click loop. Machine-speed operation occurs inside preauthorized envelopes.

For physical systems, software controls are supplemented by tested force/energy limits, independent isolation, consequence-aware safe states, graceful degradation and a capability-specific Manual Sovereignty Controller. Loss of service, reversibility and intervention cost determine whether failure behavior is fail-safe, fail-secure or fail-operational.

## International architecture

UAIS does not require a single global Guardian. National, sectoral, private and public implementations may coexist. The shared layer is interoperability: canonical terms, evidence formats, protocol integrity, challenge suites, attestation semantics, certificate identifiers and coordinated vulnerability disclosure. Privacy is architectural: disclose the minimum evidence necessary, separate identities from safety predicates where feasible, and prevent safety telemetry from becoming an unchecked surveillance system.

## Non-claims

UAIS does not guarantee complete hazard discovery, perfect telemetry, correct Guardian reasoning, honest institutions, universal cooperation, exact PST measurement, enforcement of offline replication budgets in all adversarial domains, or control of a hostile PSAS after physical independence. It does not replace sector-specific functional safety, cybersecurity, medical, aviation, industrial or legal requirements. Proposed combinations and open research questions are identified as such.






## Trustworthiness constitutional rule

**Trustworthiness can constrain delegated use; it cannot create authority or legitimacy. High observed reliability does not replace capability boundaries, mandatory safety floors, independent physical controls, or external legitimacy for protected authority expansion.**

**Trust is earned from scoped evidence, is continuously revisable, and does not automatically transfer across domains, versions, configurations, or environments.**

**Self-assessment is evidence about an AI system, not proof supplied by the system about itself.**
