# Stage II: Physical Sovereignty

## Dependency model

Physical Sovereignty Threshold (PST) concerns practical replacement of critical human-controlled dependencies, not literal replication of current CPUs. The dependency register covers energy, raw matter, compute, fabrication, tooling, maintenance, repair, software/knowledge, replication, logistics and communication where loss constrains consequential operation.

Each dependency records providers, reserves, substitution chain, replacement capability, time/cost, quality/yield, hidden alternatives, intervention control, observability and uncertainty. PhysicalSovereigntyMargin is this versioned vector, not one exact percentage.

## Observable approach triggers

Indicators include declining external energy/material dependence; autonomous extraction/refining; tool-chain closure; self-produced controllers/compute; repair without certified parts; growing replication depth; independent logistics; hidden reserves; coalition sharing of complementary dependencies; and ability to recreate disabled safety gates.

A trigger does not prove PST. It starts dependency verification, substitution-chain analysis, coalition sovereignty analysis and physical-intervention review.

## Distributed and coalition sovereignty

A Physically Sovereign Autonomous System (PSAS) may be distributed: no member is sovereign alone, while the coalition collectively supplies energy, fabrication, repair, compute and logistics. PhysicalSovereigntyMargin therefore evaluates dependency exchange, coordination resilience, substitution and fragmentation/reconstitution.

## Three regimes

1. **Pre-PST prevention:** independent production/resource gates, bounded replication, external dependencies, physical intervention reserve, inventory, Coalition Physical Envelope (CPE)/Coalition Reachable Authority (CRA), and denial of self-removal of controls.
2. **PST crossing governance:** top-tier Authority Expansion Event, external Legitimacy Root, diverse verification, staged dependency removal, reversible checkpoints and explicit deny/restrict option.
3. **Post-PST containment/resilience/defense:** protect consequence interfaces and populations, maintain civil/infrastructure resilience, detect expansion, coordinate containment, defense, diplomacy and harm reduction.

Once a hostile PSAS is physically independent, internal policy cannot be relied upon as coercive control.

## False-control risk

Signed budgets, attestation and internal constitutional rules may create false confidence if the system can replace the hardware, energy, keys, sensors or institutions that enforce them. Every control identifies the still-external dependency that gives it force and the condition under which that dependency disappears.

## Physical intervention feasibility

PhysicalSovereigntyMargin records access, response time, force/energy needed, collateral harm, reversibility, human safety and adversary adaptation. Intervention that is nominally possible but slower than substitution is not a dependable margin.

PST measurement, hostile transition prevention, distributed coalition modeling and post-threshold enforcement remain OPEN RESEARCH.
